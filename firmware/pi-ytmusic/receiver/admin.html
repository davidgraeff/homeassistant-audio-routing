Audio route checking…

This receiver sends its audio to the PipeWire Audio Router over RTP. The router only listens on a port it has been told about, so until a source exists here, casting plays to nowhere.

YouTube account checking…

Without cookies the receiver resolves anonymously: no Premium, no ad-free playback, and YouTube stops answering at all before long. With them it plays as your own signed-in account.

Login cookies

Getting a cookies.txt

Log in to music.youtube.com in a browser, then export that session with yt-dlp on your computer: 

yt-dlp --cookies-from-browser firefox:ytm \
       --cookies ~/ytm-cookies.txt \
       --skip-download --simulate \
       "https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jNQXAC9IVRw"

yt-dlp has no "just dump cookies" mode — it writes the jar as a side effect of a request, so this fetches one video's metadata and downloads nothing. Then upload ~/ytm-cookies.txt above.

Use a browser profile you then abandon. If the same login stays live in your everyday browser and here, the two rotate cookies against each other and Google invalidates both. Create a dedicated profile (firefox -CreateProfile ytm), log in, quit without logging out, and never open it again. A Private Window does not work: Firefox keeps private cookies in memory only, so there is nothing for yt-dlp to read.
What happens to the file
  • Only google.com and youtube.com cookies are kept — the rest of your browsing never leaves your computer.
  • Stored mode 0600 in the add-on's own /data, which survives restarts and updates. Cookie values are never displayed or logged.
  • It stays writable on purpose: yt-dlp refreshes the jar in place as cookies rotate, which is what keeps the session alive for weeks.
  • Nothing needs restarting — the next track picks up the new jar.
